4. Key Findings & Analysis

  • Consumption: Average annual usage fell from 1,499,347 kWh (pre-solar) to 1,190,400 kWh with solar — a 20.6% reduction (308,947 kWh/yr).
  • Rates: The average utility rate rose from $0.415/kWh pre-solar to $0.474/kWh in the solar years (+14.0%); FY26 alone averaged $0.529, with May–Jun 2026 at $0.626–$0.672.
  • Bills: FY26 spend ($615,209) rose 17.1% vs FY25 while kWh rose only 3.3% — rate increases, not usage, are now driving cost growth.
  • Savings: Solar avoided an estimated $445,089 over 3 years (FY24 $94,383, FY25 $173,465, FY26 $177,241). Savings grow as rates rise, since each avoided kWh is worth more.
  • Payback: 62.8% of the $708,635 investment is recovered; at $148,363/yr, simple payback is ~4.8 years (around May 2028), well inside the 15-year depreciable life.
  • Seasonality: The largest monthly reduction is in February (−34.4%); the smallest is in March (−3.3%) — review the high-usage months (e.g., Dec 2023, Mar 2024) for load or system-output issues.
  • Profitability: After depreciation of $47,242/yr, solar still yields an average net benefit of $101,121/yr (simple ROI 20.9% per year).
  • Recommendations: (1) Track the new rate tier monthly and update savings estimates; (2) investigate FY24 underperformance vs FY25–26; (3) consider efficiency measures or added capacity to offset continued rate increases.
